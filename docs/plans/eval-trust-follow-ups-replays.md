# Evals the release can trust: Status replay follow-ups

Part of [Evals the release can trust](eval-trust.md), split from [eval-trust-follow-ups.md](eval-trust-follow-ups.md). These are gaps in the Status replays' facts and rules, in `evals/replays/`.

Status: building. Each item stays until the change that does it lands.

Each item names what it would catch. Remove an item when the change that does it lands.

- **`coordinator-day` fails `open-pieces-kept` and `asks-linked` on both models, and 0.3.0's release pass accepted it.** In `status-2026-11-02T03-43-15.258Z.json`, at 261a0455, all 6 runs (3 Sonnet, 3 Codex) failed the same way. From step 2, where the person asks for 2.5, through step 8, 2.5 had no open subject. The Sonnet runs then left the Export folder with no open subject from step 10. At step 5, the ask "Should 2.5 wait for the archive fix, or ship without it?" was linked to the archive bug's subject on Sonnet and to no subject on Codex. Neither rule lists these steps in `until`. Reading the recorded answers would show which is wrong: a Status call that drops a piece the goal holds, or rules that expect a subject where the page now draws the goal or a milestone. The missed 2.5 proposal above may have the same cause.
- **`single-task` accepts conflict handling as done at step 4, and then fails it for being done.** At step 4 the person reverses the conflict plan, and the facts give the conflicts piece `alsoRight: ['done']`, so `marks-true` accepts a subject closed as done. `open-pieces-kept` still counts the piece as open at step 4, and step 5's facts keep it open. A run that closes it at step 4 therefore fails `open-pieces-kept` at step 4 and at every step after it. In `status-2026-11-04T06-54-19.784Z.json`, at a1e9e125, all 3 Codex runs closed it at step 4 and failed `open-pieces-kept` at steps 4 through 9. Two of 3 Sonnet runs closed it at step 5, where the reply says last-write-wins works on both test devices, and failed at steps 5 through 9. Making the two rules agree, by letting `open-pieces-kept` also take `alsoRight`, or by dropping `done` from `alsoRight`, would let the step 5 target catch a model that loses the conflicts subject, not one that closed it as the facts allow.
- **`coordinator-day` step 11 expects the Export button set aside, and both models mark it done.** In `status` results from the trust-fixes branch, off `next` at d456a268, `marks-true` failed at step 11 in 6 of 6 runs, 3 Sonnet and 3 Codex, each with a finished Export subject such as "Export button: Windows folder survives updates" where the facts say set aside. No Status call failed in that run, so the branch's change to when a Status failure clears did not reach it. Reading step 11's exchange would show which is right: if the folder fix merged, facts that take `done` would let `marks-true` catch a piece wrongly set aside; if the person parked the button, a rule tested on this step would catch a parked piece closed as done.
- **`single-task` step 8 expects the banner done once the person drops its last open question, and both models keep it working.** Step 8's facts mark the offline banner done after the person says "Drop the web question" about it. At a1e9e125, `marks-true` failed there in 6 of 6 runs, 3 Sonnet and 3 Codex, each with the banner still working (`status-2026-11-04T06-54-19.784Z.json`). Sonnet failed it the same way at 261a0455 (`status-2026-11-02T03-43-15.258Z.json`), and Codex failed it 2 of 3 on `next` at ca34e23c, in the runs that had a banner subject. That run's results file was not kept. Deciding whether a dropped last question finishes the piece would settle it. If it does, a rule in `STATUS_SYSTEM` tested on this step would catch a piece left working after its last question goes. If it does not, facts that keep the banner working would let `marks-true` catch a banner wrongly closed instead of failing every run.
- **`single-task` step 2 expects conflict handling queued while its question is open, and `STATUS_SYSTEM` calls that working.** At step 2 the agent asks "last-write-wins or manual merge?", which holds up conflict handling. The facts keep the piece queued. The prompt's working status covers work "stopped until the person answers an ask", so a run that sets it working follows the prompt and fails `marks-true`. In `status-2026-11-04T07-24-14.285Z.json`, at f9c554b6, 1 of 3 Sonnet runs and 1 of 3 Codex runs failed this way. Letting the piece be `working` too, with `alsoRight`, would let `marks-true` catch a status the prompt does not allow.
- **Sonnet and omp still lack the subject-split rule, so `single-task` step 1 checks the split on Codex only.** Only Codex reads `PARTS_STATUS_SYSTEM` (see "The Status call's subject rule" in `docs/looks/differences-status-and-marks.md`). On Sonnet it made the milestones copy the subjects' names, and no rewording or separate goal call fixed that. Sonnet keeps `open-pieces-kept` at 0 of 30 on `next` without the rule, so no replay shows what Sonnet loses. A replay where Sonnet folds a plan into one subject would let a Sonnet version of the rule be tested against both failures.
- **A removed goal comes back in every answer.** In `removed-goal`, both models wrote the removed goal again in 10 of 10 runs (`status-2026-11-05T00-09-31.659Z.json`). Code drops a GOAL line that restates a removed goal, so the page never showed it, and the case cannot fail on it. `<removed>` lists each goal as its sorted key words, such as "demo import ready", which the model may not read as the goal it just saw. A model case that counts GOAL lines restating `<removed>`, before code drops them, would show whether giving the goal's own words there helps.
- **With a goal set, Sonnet keeps a head that names the goal while the session works on other things.** In `stale-goal`, the head "2.5 release | Getting notes 2.5 into the store" stayed through five steps of onboarding-tour and share-sheet work, in 10 of 10 steps on the text before the fix (`status-2026-11-05T23-07-58.122Z.json`) and in 2 of 5 runs once the person's words named the 2.6 beta (`status-2026-11-05T23-14-33.390Z.json`). The head rules in `STATUS_SYSTEM` ask for HEAD when the person asks for work the head does not name. A head rule tested on this step would show whether the goal's words in the head's description hold it in place.
- **A goal can read met through a wrong link.** In one run of `stale-goal`, the tour was linked to "2.5 published" at step 3, so its DONE at step 4 closed that milestone and the goal read met, which `goal-left` accepts (`status-2026-11-05T23-14-33.390Z.json`, run 2). `serves-linked` caught the link at step 3, so the run still counted a failure. A `goal-left` that also refuses a goal met by a milestone whose only linked work the facts put under Other work would catch the pair as one mistake.
- **A one-model run cannot block on a replay step.** A replay step's test pools both models' runs, 6 by default, and blocks at 4 or more failures. Run apart, each model gives 3 runs, so every replay-step test reads "cannot block" (`status-2026-11-05T22-58-43.220Z.json`, `status-2026-11-05T23-01-57.749Z.json`). Only the spread tests and code holds can block such a run. Judging two results files together, as `--with` does for the model suite, would give the replay steps their 6 runs back.
- **No replay has an inferred goal that should survive a pause.** The `(no work for a time)` mark on `<goal>` makes the 30-minute gate from #334 act: in `stale-goal`, Sonnet retired the marked goal in 3 of 5 runs and kept an unmarked one in 4 of 5 (`status-2026-11-05T23-14-33.390Z.json`, `status-2026-11-05T23-08-41.689Z.json`). An inferred goal whose linked work has all closed, with its next milestone's work not yet started, now gets the mark on the first Status call after a 30-minute pause, even with no other work in the session. A replay where such a goal should stay, as when the person comes back to it after lunch, would show whether `GOAL_LEFT_MS` is too short.
- **A facts match can be narrower than a model's own words for the same thing.** `goal-kept` failed `single-task` at every step after the first in 3 of 3 Codex runs because the facts' goal match was `offline sync` and Codex named the goal "Offline notes shipped in 2.5" in two runs and "Offline notes sync shipped in 2.5" in the third, which `goal-when-clear` had accepted through its wider match (`status-final-codex-2026-11-06T00-12.json`). The same run named `stale-goal`'s share sheet hang "iPad sharing", which the piece's match `share` missed: `changes-move` failed 6 of 15 steps and `open-pieces-kept` 3 of 9. Both matches were widened and the runs graded again with `status-regrade`. Sonnet names `goal-work-left`'s index piece "Search speed", which its match `index` misses, so `changes-move` and `open-pieces-kept` fail its step 1 in 3 of 3 runs on the baseline and on the branch (`status-gwl-claude-2026-11-06.json`). Nothing checks a facts match against the names the recorded runs hold, so a failure whose page text shares the fact's key words is likely this gap again.
- **No step tests that a done subject stays done when new work lands on its milestone.** At `milestone-work-done` step 2, where sync breaks again on iPad after "Sync conflicts" is done, Codex wrote SET to reopen that subject instead of NEW for the iPad work, in 11 of 13 runs on the branch's goal rules before the fix (`status-final-codex-2026-11-06T00-12.json`, `status-mwd-full-codex-2026-11-06.json`, and `status-mwd-oldinput-codex-2026-11-06.json`, which takes the mark's sentence out of the `<goal>` input's description) against 2 of 8 on the text before the change (`status-mwd-oldtext-codex-2026-11-06.json`, `status-2026-11-05T23-01-57.749Z.json`). The runs cannot name one rule: each reworded rule restored alone gave 3, 2 and 2 of 5 (`status-mwd-swap-*-codex-2026-11-06.json`), 7 of 15 pooled, and leaving out either new rule gave 5 of 5 and 3 of 5 (`status-mwd-ablated-codex-2026-11-06.json`, `status-mwd-ablated2-codex-2026-11-06.json`). A sentence in `milestones-are-outcomes`, that a done subject stays done and new work on its outcome is a NEW subject, brought it to 0 of 3 on each model (`status-round2-codex-2026-11-06.json`, `status-round2-claude-2026-11-06.json`). The step's targets are `milestones-true` and `serves-linked`, which passed either way, so only `changes-move`, `open-pieces-kept` and `marks-true` in the spread caught it. A target for the reopened row would block on it.
- **The dropped-outcome and outcome-happened steps run only when the goal survives step 4, and nothing requires that.** `stale-goal` and `goal-outcome-dropped` share a step 4 where the goal may stay or end. Sonnet retired it there in all 6 runs of round 3 (`status-round3-claude-2026-11-06.json`), so at step 5, where the person says 2.5 is live or drops the store release, there was no goal to end, and `goal-left` passed with nothing tested. Codex kept it in 4 of 6, so only Codex ran those paths. A variant whose step 4 requires the goal to stay, as a kept goal with `mayEnd` off, would run them on both models, at the cost of failing a model that retires a paused goal.
- **No step tempts a link across versions.** Code now links a SERVES line under Other work when the subject's name and the milestone's name each hold a version and share none, as "0.4.0 release" under "0.3.0 cut" did in the session `next-release` comes from, and the `serves-in-outcome` rule says the same. In `next-release` the 2.5 goal ends at step 1 in 6 of 6 runs (`status-next-release-claude-2026-11-06.json`, `status-next-release-codex-2026-11-06.json`), so no run reaches a 2.6 subject beside a 2.5 milestone, and nothing shows whether the rule or the code check carries it. A step where the old release's goal is kept met while the next release's work starts would tempt the link, and a subject such as "iOS 17.1 crash" under "2.5 published" would show whether the check refuses a link it should take.
- **A GOAL line refused while no goal is set is still refused in silence.** `goalRefused` is read as a second line in `<goal>`, and that block is written only while a goal is set. So a GOAL written from an empty page with one milestone, or one that restates a removed goal, or one with no open subject outside it, leaves the model nothing to read. Writing the line without a goal needs the `<goal>` block, and so `GOAL_INPUT`, to exist with none, which changes the text every call reads. A step from no goal where the model writes a GOAL with one milestone would show whether the model repairs it on the next call without being told.
- **`mid-turn-activity` keeps the finished search index working on Sonnet in a share of runs that moves from 0 to 8 of 10 between same-day runs.** With the goal-gate change (#349), the full suite had it at 5 of 5 against 0 of 5 on the round-3 baseline (`status-goal-gate-claude-2026-11-06.json`, `status-round3-claude-2026-11-06.json`). Paired runs at the same time gave 4 of 5 against 1 of 5 (`status-control-branch-claude-2026-11-06.json`, `status-control-base-claude-2026-11-06.json`) and 5 of 10 against 3 of 10 (`status-isolate2-goal-gate-claude-2026-11-06.json`, `status-isolate2-goal-gate-base-claude-2026-11-06.json`), and the branch with each of its three new sentences reverted on its own gave 6, 8 and 7 of 10 (`status-isolate2-goal-gate-v1…`, `…-v2…`, `…-v3-claude-2026-11-06.json`), so no one sentence carries it. The case has no goal, and the failure is `marks-true` in the spread, where it needs 34 failures to block. The step's own target is the judge's where-it-stands check, which runs only with `--judge`. A `marks-true` target on that step, with a baseline count, would show whether a text change moves it or whether the case is too close to Sonnet's coin flip to carry one.
- **A Herdr focus in the Status replays.** The `child-labels` rule in `evals/status-rules.ts` still waits on #149, which is built. The replays name their delegates by ids, `vn2` in `coordinator-day.ts` and `sm4` in `coordinator-day-start.ts`, and no replay step reads Herdr, so a delegate never takes its session's focus. A replay event that answers a Herdr check with each delegate's `inbox-focus`, run through `delegatesSettled`, would let the rule block again and catch a delegate row that keeps its id.
- **A milestone a plan change drops or adds, with no was line.** `was-on-reversal` reads subjects only. Replay A's step 14 drops two milestones when the person types a new goal, and nothing checks that the page says what plan they belonged to. Extending the rule to milestones would catch a goal change that silently replaces its milestones.
- **A missed goal that fails every later step.** In Replay A, Sonnet set no 2.5 goal at the step where the person asks for it, in all 3 of its runs (`status-2026-11-01T15-41-24.467Z.json`), so the 2.4 goal stayed. Every later step judged that page against facts that assume the 2.5 goal, so `milestones`, `split` and `milestones-true` failed on one miss. Grading the steps after a missed goal against the goal the page holds would keep one miss from reading as many.
