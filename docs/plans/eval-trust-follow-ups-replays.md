# Evals the release can trust: Status replay follow-ups

Part of [Evals the release can trust](eval-trust.md), split from [eval-trust-follow-ups.md](eval-trust-follow-ups.md). These are gaps in the Status replays' facts and rules, in `evals/replays/`.

Status: building. Each item stays until the change that does it lands.

Each item names what it would catch. Remove an item when the change that does it lands.

- **`coordinator-day` fails `open-pieces-kept` and `asks-linked` on both models, and 0.3.0's release pass accepted it.** In `status-2026-11-02T03-43-15.258Z.json`, at 261a0455, all 6 runs (3 Sonnet, 3 Codex) failed the same way. From step 2, where the person asks for 2.5, through step 8, 2.5 had no open subject. The Sonnet runs then left the Export folder with no open subject from step 10. At step 5, the ask "Should 2.5 wait for the archive fix, or ship without it?" was linked to the archive bug's subject on Sonnet and to no subject on Codex. Neither rule lists these steps in `until`. Reading the recorded answers would show which is wrong: a Status call that drops a piece the goal holds, or rules that expect a subject where the page now draws the goal or a milestone. The missed 2.5 proposal above may have the same cause.
- **`single-task` accepts conflict handling as done at step 4, and then fails it for being done.** At step 4 the person reverses the conflict plan, and the facts give the conflicts piece `alsoRight: ['done']`, so `marks-true` accepts a subject closed as done. `open-pieces-kept` still counts the piece as open at step 4, and step 5's facts keep it open. A run that closes it at step 4 therefore fails `open-pieces-kept` at step 4 and at every step after it. In `status-2026-11-04T06-54-19.784Z.json`, at a1e9e125, all 3 Codex runs closed it at step 4 and failed `open-pieces-kept` at steps 4 through 9. Two of 3 Sonnet runs closed it at step 5, where the reply says last-write-wins works on both test devices, and failed at steps 5 through 9. Making the two rules agree, by letting `open-pieces-kept` also take `alsoRight`, or by dropping `done` from `alsoRight`, would let the step 5 target catch a model that loses the conflicts subject, not one that closed it as the facts allow.
- **`coordinator-day` step 11 expects the Export button set aside, and both models mark it done.** In `status` results from the trust-fixes branch, off `next` at d456a268, `marks-true` failed at step 11 in 6 of 6 runs, 3 Sonnet and 3 Codex, each with a finished Export subject such as "Export button: Windows folder survives updates" where the facts say set aside. No Status call failed in that run, so the branch's change to when a Status failure clears did not reach it. Reading step 11's exchange would show which is right: if the folder fix merged, facts that take `done` would let `marks-true` catch a piece wrongly set aside; if the person parked the button, a rule tested on this step would catch a parked piece closed as done.
- **`single-task` step 8 expects the banner done once the person drops its last open question, and both models keep it working.** Step 8's facts mark the offline banner done after the person says "Drop the web question" about it. At a1e9e125, `marks-true` failed there in 6 of 6 runs, 3 Sonnet and 3 Codex, each with the banner still working (`status-2026-11-04T06-54-19.784Z.json`). Sonnet failed it the same way at 261a0455 (`status-2026-11-02T03-43-15.258Z.json`), and Codex failed it 2 of 3 on `next` at ca34e23c, in the runs that had a banner subject. That run's results file was not kept. Deciding whether a dropped last question finishes the piece would settle it. If it does, a rule in `STATUS_SYSTEM` tested on this step would catch a piece left working after its last question goes. If it does not, facts that keep the banner working would let `marks-true` catch a banner wrongly closed instead of failing every run.
- **`single-task` step 2 expects conflict handling queued while its question is open, and `STATUS_SYSTEM` calls that working.** At step 2 the agent asks "last-write-wins or manual merge?", which holds up conflict handling. The facts keep the piece queued. The prompt's working status covers work "stopped until the person answers an ask", so a run that sets it working follows the prompt and fails `marks-true`. In `status-2026-11-04T07-24-14.285Z.json`, at f9c554b6, 1 of 3 Sonnet runs and 1 of 3 Codex runs failed this way. Letting the piece be `working` too, with `alsoRight`, would let `marks-true` catch a status the prompt does not allow.
- **Sonnet and omp still lack the subject-split rule, so `single-task` step 1 checks the split on Codex only.** Only Codex reads `PARTS_STATUS_SYSTEM` (see "The Status call's subject rule" in `docs/looks/differences-status-and-marks.md`). On Sonnet it made the milestones copy the subjects' names, and no rewording or separate goal call fixed that. Sonnet keeps `open-pieces-kept` at 0 of 30 on `next` without the rule, so no replay shows what Sonnet loses. A replay where Sonnet folds a plan into one subject would let a Sonnet version of the rule be tested against both failures.
- **A removed goal comes back in every answer.** In `removed-goal`, both models wrote the removed goal again in 10 of 10 runs (`status-2026-11-05T00-09-31.659Z.json`). Code drops a GOAL line that restates a removed goal, so the page never showed it, and the case cannot fail on it. `<removed>` lists each goal as its sorted key words, such as "demo import ready", which the model may not read as the goal it just saw. A model case that counts GOAL lines restating `<removed>`, before code drops them, would show whether giving the goal's own words there helps.
- **With a goal set, Sonnet keeps a head that names the goal while the session works on other things.** In `stale-goal`, the head "2.5 release | Getting notes 2.5 into the store" stayed through five steps of onboarding-tour and share-sheet work, in 10 of 10 steps on the text before the fix (`status-2026-11-05T23-07-58.122Z.json`) and in 2 of 5 runs once the person's words named the 2.6 beta (`status-2026-11-05T23-14-33.390Z.json`). The head rules in `STATUS_SYSTEM` ask for HEAD when the person asks for work the head does not name. A head rule tested on this step would show whether the goal's words in the head's description hold it in place.
- **A goal can read met through a wrong link.** In one run of `stale-goal`, the tour was linked to "2.5 published" at step 3, so its DONE at step 4 closed that milestone and the goal read met, which `goal-left` accepts (`status-2026-11-05T23-14-33.390Z.json`, run 2). `serves-linked` caught the link at step 3, so the run still counted a failure. A `goal-left` that also refuses a goal met by a milestone whose only linked work the facts put under Other work would catch the pair as one mistake.
- **A one-model run cannot block on a replay step.** A replay step's test pools both models' runs, 6 by default, and blocks at 4 or more failures. Run apart, as the 220-call cap requires, each model gives 3 runs, so every replay-step test reads "cannot block" (`status-2026-11-05T22-58-43.220Z.json`, `status-2026-11-05T23-01-57.749Z.json`). Only the spread tests and code holds can block such a run. Judging two results files together, as `--with` does for the model suite, would give the replay steps their 6 runs back.
- **`goal-on-busy-page` expects two borderline links.** Its facts say "Live cursors" serves "Live edits merge" and "Feedback form" serves "Testers invited". Under the `serves-in-outcome` rule, which links a subject only when finishing it is part of the milestone's named outcome, `-` fits both as well: cursors are not part of edits merging, and a form is not part of inviting testers. With that rule, Sonnet put one or both under Other work in 4 of 10 runs (`status-2026-11-05T23-15-15.628Z.json`, `status-2026-11-05T23-23-39.630Z.json`), against 0 of 10 on the text before it. A `serves` fact that allows both readings for a borderline piece would let the case count only links that are wrong under either reading.
