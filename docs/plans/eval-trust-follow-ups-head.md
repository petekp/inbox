# Evals the release can trust: Status head rule follow-ups

Part of [Evals the release can trust](eval-trust.md), split from [eval-trust-follow-ups-replays.md](eval-trust-follow-ups-replays.md). These are gaps in the rules that check the Status head, in `evals/status-rules-head.ts`, and in the replay facts they read.

Status: building. Each item names what it would catch. Remove an item when the change that does it lands.

- **`head-no-status` fails a head description that names the work under way.** Its word list in `evals/status-rule-helpers.ts` holds "merged", "ready" and "shipped" in any form of words. In `restart-reads-backlog`, 16 of 20 Sonnet runs wrote "Getting three draft PRs checked and merged", which says what the session is doing. The check failed all 16 and blocked the replay (`status-2026-11-17T00-32-16.830Z.json`). A check that fails a word only where it reports a state, such as "PRs merged", and passes it after "getting" or "until", would catch a head that says where one piece stands without failing one that names the work.
- **`head-follows` fails a description rewrite that drops work the same step finished.** In `goal-outcome-dropped` step 1, the crash fix merges while the tour goes on, and Sonnet rewrote "Fixing launch crash, redoing onboarding tour" as "Redoing the onboarding tour". Decision 22 asks for that correction. The rule counts it as a head that moved while the focus held, since only filling an empty description is exempt. Exempting a rewrite whose old description names a subject the step closed would let the right answer pass, and still catch a rewrite to the current piece, such as "Speeding up note search" turned into "Putting newest notes first in results".
- **Low priority: `head-follows` and `head-title` split one mistake at `coordinator-day` step 7.** In 24 recorded runs, each failed exactly one of the two. A head description that still names the merged fix fails `head-follows`, and dropping the description fails `head-title`. `head-follows` has an `until` there, so no verdict changes, but the spread counts one mistake under two rules. One check of the head's description at that step would count it once.
- **`head-after-done` step 2 also grades step 1's answer.** Once code clears the description, `head-follows` fails a vague step 1 title such as "Command utilities". `head-no-finished` fails a run whose step 1 left the finished `--repeat` working. Every step 2 failure in `status-2026-11-19T12-58-00.353Z.json` came from one of the two. A step 2 that starts from a fixed step 1 page would catch only a finishing answer that names finished work.
- **`goal-wait` fails `head-follows` in every run, and the rule blocks when the case runs alone.** The case's focus is the 2.5 release, so the rule wants a head about "2.5" or "store". Every model moves the head to the work the step starts, such as "Share sheet | Making the share sheet open faster". `goal-wait` failed it in 6 of 6 runs in both 0.6.0 release runs and on the `status-fixes` branch, and in 18 of 18 in `status-2026-11-20T22-28-22.314Z.json`, a `--replay goal-wait --runs 9` run. `head-follows` is a spread rule judged over a whole run, so in a full run the other replays hide it, and a run of `goal-wait` alone blocks on it. Facts for this step that accept a head about the new work, or a `head-follows` target that only this step runs, would let a run of `goal-wait` alone pass when the case is right, and fail a head that names neither the release nor the step's work.
- **`next-milestone` fails `head-follows` on Claude in most runs, so a run of it alone blocks.** At step 1 the person lays out 2.4: the conflicts, then the export button, then the release. Claude retitles the head from "Sync conflicts" to "2.4 release", which names the whole plan. The rule counts that as a head that moved while the focus held. At step 3 Claude keeps "2.4 release" with no description, and the rule wants the migration. It failed 9 of 9 runs in `status-2026-11-23T16-53-03.263Z.json`, and 7 of 9 on the prompt before it in `status-2026-11-23T16-54-18.253Z.json`. Facts that accept a head naming the release the plan builds would pass the step 1 answer, and still fail a head retitled to one piece.
- **Sonnet retitles the head to the fix in `goal-work-unnamed`, more often with the `serves-in-outcome` and `goal-suggest` sentences from #986.** The person says the progress bar has to work before the demo, and Sonnet writes a head such as "Fixing the progress bar, then the demo script". That fails `head-follows`, and often `head-no-queued`. It failed 7 of 15 Sonnet runs on `next`'s text (`status-2026-11-26T23-52-44.358Z.json`), 11 of 15 on #986's text (`status-2026-11-27T00-09-38.522Z.json`) and 5 of 5 in the full run at fb08771b (`status-2026-11-27T00-37-23.273Z.json`). Codex failed none. The head rules are judged over the whole run, so the case passes. A `head-follows` target on this step would block a text change that makes the head follow the piece being worked on.
